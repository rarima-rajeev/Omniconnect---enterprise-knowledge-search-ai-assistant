## Your first week

On day one you will receive your laptop, Okta account and GitHub access. Your onboarding buddy will set up a 30-minute check-in every day for your first two weeks. By the end of week one you should have cloned the monorepo, run the test suite locally and shipped a small change, usually a documentation fix, through the full review and deploy process.

## Development environment

We use a single monorepo hosted on GitHub. Most services are written in TypeScript (Node 20) and Go 1.22. Run `make bootstrap` to install toolchains through mise, then `make dev` to start the local stack in Docker Compose. The local stack includes Postgres, Redis and a fake identity provider with test users.

Never connect your local environment to production data. Sanitized staging snapshots are available on request through the #data-access channel.

## Code review

Every change needs one approving review from a code owner. Keep pull requests small: under 400 changed lines is the norm. Write a description that explains why the change is needed, not only what changed. Reviewers aim to respond within one business day.

## Deploys

We deploy continuously. Merging to main triggers CI, which runs unit tests, integration tests and a security scan. Passing builds deploy to staging automatically and to production after a 30-minute soak with automated canary analysis. You can watch your deploy in the #deploys channel.

## On-call

Engineers join the on-call rotation for their team after three months. Rotations are one week long, with a primary and a secondary. On-call engineers receive a weekly stipend and a day off after any night with a page between midnight and 6 a.m. The SRE team in Bengaluru provides follow-the-sun first response for production alerts.

## Learning resources

Recorded architecture talks are in the Engineering Learning library on the intranet. New engineers should watch "How a query flows through the gateway" and "Our permission model" in their first month. Each engineer has a $1,500 annual learning budget for courses, books and conferences.

## Who to ask

Ask questions in your team channel first. For build or tooling problems use #eng-help. For access requests use #data-access. Your onboarding buddy and manager are always the right people to ask when you are unsure.
